The history of computing hardware spans the development of devices from simple mechanical aids to modern electronic computers. Early calculating tools such as the abacus assisted with arithmetic for thousands of years. In the nineteenth century, Charles Babbage designed the analytical engine, a mechanical general-purpose computer that introduced concepts such as the stored program and conditional branching. Ada Lovelace, who worked with Babbage, is often regarded as the first computer programmer because she wrote an algorithm intended to be processed by the machine. The twentieth century saw rapid progress: vacuum tubes gave way to transistors, and transistors to integrated circuits, each step dramatically reducing size and cost while increasing speed and reliability. The invention of the microprocessor in the early nineteen seventies placed an entire central processing unit on a single chip, enabling the personal computer revolution that followed. Today, graphics processing units accelerate not only rendering but also the massively parallel arithmetic at the heart of modern machine learning, training neural networks with billions of parameters on enormous datasets.
