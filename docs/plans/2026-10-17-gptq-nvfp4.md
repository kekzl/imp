# GPTQ for NVFP4 exports

Roadmap row 101, 2026-10-17. Question: does GPTQ error feedback on top of the NVFP4 export (16-element micro-blocks, FP8 E4M3 scales) close more of the BF16 gap than AWQ alone?

## Findings

- At damp 0.01 every one of the 196 Qwen3-1.7B tensors drops to 0.21-0.37 of the RTN proxy loss, and PPL still rises 15 % over RTN. Hessians taken on the eval corpus itself give the same result (21.94), so this is not calibration overfit.
- PPL falls monotonically as damp grows to 100. The proxy loss models the BF16 inputs, but at run time the inputs carry the error of the earlier quantized layers. GPTQ at small damp puts the weight error exactly into the low-variance directions that this noise hits.
- Recalibrating with an H that differs only in run-to-run noise (proxy 73902 vs 73904) moves AWQ+GPTQ damp 10 from 18.05 to 18.30. Across damp 5-30 the spread is 18.07-18.46 around AWQ's 18.23: no measurable gain on Qwen3-1.7B.
- On Qwen3-0.6B all 7 AWQ+GPTQ points (27.02-27.40) sit below AWQ's 27.59, a mean of -1.5 %.
- A row-norm outlier filter (rows above 20x the per-call median left out of H) dropped 1 of 1521743 rows. RMSNorm equalizes row norms, so it was removed.

Decision: `--gptq` ships opt-in with default damp 10 (`gptq::kDefaultDamp`). It is not part of the default `--calib` recipe. Row 101 stays open for the levers that change the error distribution itself: rotation (MR-GPTQ, Hadamard before the 16-blocks) and sequential calibration against quantized inputs.

## 2026-11-01: GPTAQ (step 1b)

GPTAQ (Li et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2504.02692) fits ||W X - Wq Xq|| with H = Xq Xq^T from the quantized model's inputs and adds W P to every GPTQ update, P = alpha * triu1(dXX U^T) U, dXX = (X - Xq) Xq^T, U the upper Cholesky factor of H^-1 (the reference code scales P by alpha 0.25).

| Step | Where |
|---|---|
| BF16 pass: `--set calibration.pair_dump=D` writes every weight input [rows, K] FP16 after warmup (150k-char corpus: 2016 records on Qwen3-0.6B and 1.7B each) | `src/exec/activation_calibrator_pair.cpp` |
| AWQ export pass over the same corpus: `--set calibration.pair_read=D` reads record n of (layer, kind) at its n-th call, refuses any shape mismatch or unread record, writes `F.gptaq` (IMPHES02: mean xq^T xq and mean x^T xq) | same, `src/quant/calibration_hessian.h` |
| `imp-quantize --calib <bf16 stats> --gptaq F.gptaq [--gptq-damp X] [--gptaq-alpha A]`: H used as is (already x / s), dXX = diag(1/s) C - H | `tools/imp-quantize/quantize_weight.cpp`, `gptq.cu` |
| A/B: `tools/analysis/gptaq_ab.sh` | |

## Findings (GPTAQ)

- REFUTED: acceptance was >= 2 % below AWQ on both models at one damp value. Only 0.6B damp 1 alpha 0.05 passes (-2.31 %); on 1.7B every GPTAQ arm is above AWQ, and PPL rises with alpha at both damps on both models.
- The rounding lottery bounds every verdict here: a kernel change that moved only float rounding (one runtime branch in `column_kernel`, proxy loss equal to 6 digits) moved 0.6B GPTQ damp 10 from 27.2857 to 27.1658 and GPTAQ alpha 0 damp 10 from 27.3539 to 26.9836 (1.4 %). The shipped kernel keeps GPTQ bit-identical to the previous binary.
- On a synthetic input (`GptqNvfp4.AsymmetricTermTracksTheFullPrecisionOutput`) the term does what the paper says: output error vs the full-precision reference falls monotonically from GPTQ to alpha 1.
- Hypothesis, not tested: the paper collects Xq sequentially (layer l sees inputs of layers < l already quantized by GPTAQ); here every layer sees the RTN-rounded AWQ export, so each layer compensates upstream error that the upstream layers also compensate. A sequential run needs one export pass per layer.

Decision: `--gptaq` and `calibration.pair_*` stay in, opt-in, documented as measured without gain. Row 101 stays open: sequential GPTAQ, or export-side Had16.

## 2026-11-01: sequential GPTAQ

## Findings (rotation)

- REFUTED on both models: every rotated arm is far above AWQ (best rotated arm 0.6B `full_vo` AWQ+GPTQ +57.17 %, 1.7B `full` +74.36 %). The BF16 gate holds (worst +0.52 %), so the rotation is exact and the loss is quantization.
- The loss is on both sides: weight-only W4A16 rises +45.89 % under `full`, W4A4 +120.06 %. NVFP4's per-16 micro-scales already isolate outlier channels and rows; a rotation spreads them into every group. Qwen3 norm gammas reach 61x their median (0.6B `post_attention_layernorm` layer 27: max 192, median 3.125), and folding them alone costs +5.25 % W4A16 / +11.89 % W4A4 RTN on 0.6B.
- Rotation also makes FP16 rounding visible: `had16` written as F16 is +0.52 % over the source with no quantization at all. Hypothesis, not tested: the large outlier component that rotation mixes into every coordinate scales every per-element error, rounding or FP4.
- `had16` is worse than `full` on 0.6B (286 vs 66 AWQ), the same direction as MR-GPTQ's NVFP4 tables, at a much larger magnitude on 0.6B than their 8B drop.

Decision: no `imp-quantize` option. `rotate_residual.py` (self-tested) and `rotate_ab.sh` stay in `tools/analysis/` as the harness. Training-free export levers measured for row 101: micro-scale search (#1083), GPTQ, GPTQ on the export's H, GPTAQ, sequential GPTAQ, residual rotation (`had16`, `full`, `full_vo`). None reaches AWQ - 2 % on both models; closest is sequential GPTAQ alpha 0 damp 10 (-2.91 % / -0.55 %). Left: mixed precision (FP8 for the most sensitive tensors, trades size) or block-wise reconstruction / QAT (L).
